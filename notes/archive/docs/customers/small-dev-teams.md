# Segment 4 — Small dev teams

**Priority: FOURTH for revenue. FIRST for distribution. Read that twice.**

## Who

2–10 engineers adopting agentic tooling — Claude Code, agent skills, MCP servers, subagents.
Often a tech lead who has taken it on themselves to figure this out for everyone.

## Economics — the honest version

- **Medium willingness to pay.** They will pay, but they benchmark everything against free.
- They can build it themselves, and a meaningful fraction will, out of principle.
- They expect open source and treat paid content in this space with suspicion.
- They will argue about the price **in public**, which is a cost the other segments don't impose.

## Why they are still in scope

They are the **cheapest audience to reach and the loudest to amplify**. A genuinely good free
artifact aimed at this segment travels through X, HN, Reddit and Discord in a way that nothing
aimed at segments 1–3 ever will.

So we target them deliberately — with **free** work — and let that reach sell to the segments
that pay. This is a marketing budget, not a revenue line. Judge it on reach, not conversion.

## Their actual pain

Real and recurring:

- Agent tooling changes monthly; their configs rot silently.
- No good patterns for team-shared skills, subagents, or MCP setup.
- Every team reinvents the same handful of workflows badly.
- Nobody owns keeping any of it current.

That last one is exactly what we sell. Which means this segment *could* convert — via
maintenance, never via content.

## What they WILL pay for

- Maintenance and update guarantees.
- Team sync and shared configuration — the Phase 3 SaaS, if it happens.
- Not the artifacts themselves.

## How to reach them

- GitHub (free repo as the top of funnel), X, Hacker News, relevant Discords.

## The trap to avoid

Mistaking their engagement for traction. Stars, upvotes and replies from this segment are
**reach metrics, not demand metrics.** If the metrics dashboard is green on engagement and
flat on revenue, this is almost certainly why.
